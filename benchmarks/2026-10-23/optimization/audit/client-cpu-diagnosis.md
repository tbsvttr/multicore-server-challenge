# Generator accounting and preserved invalid attempt

The first capacity study, `capacity_20261023t095453_5574c0`, produced no capacity score. Rust passed its 10,000-user warmup and 80,000-user probe, but the 160,000-user attempt was **INVALID_GENERATOR**: the external client exhausted99.808% of its four-CPU quota, had genuine58–86-second sampler delays, and failed to activate99% of its users. The target stayed healthy, used18.641% of its eight-CPU budget during that invalid window, and had no OOM. Its46,578 measured timeouts are retained as invalid-attempt data; they do not establish a Rust capacity boundary. The complete native verdict, clocks, monitors and histograms remain unchanged.

The operator stopped the whole study after identifying that client bottleneck. Goose’s subsequent warmup was interrupted before its measurement window; `INCOMPLETE` records that interruption, rather than an application failure. Both owned applications and temporary databases were cleaned up. See `audit/capacity-first-study-stop.json` and `audit/capacity-first-result.json`.

The native client’s CPU sampler reads the aggregate `/proc/stat` counter (`bench/cmd/loadgen/sysstat.go`), and its merge command flags boxCPU>75% (`merge.go`). Inside Docker on this Mac, that counter includes the server and PostgreSQL in the same Linux VM. It cannot measure the generator’s CPU headroom independently.

The fresh preregistered **local v2** study therefore gives the external client8CPU/8GiB/no swap and measures its own cgroup CPU, requiring≤75% utilization with complete valid monitoring. The app and PostgreSQL still share8CPU/16GiB/no swap. Every native timing, active-user, latency and error rule remains mandatory; the native workload binary and full seed are unchanged. Only a whole-VM CPU flag can differ between native and independent local qualification. Native verdicts and flags are preserved verbatim, and scored holds report both qualifications. Genuine stalls, unknown/mixed reasons, CPU-summary tampering, client quota saturation, incomplete coverage, clocks and OOM still invalidate the phase. Tests explicitly confirm that the earlier160,000-user attempt remains invalid under the new policy.

The new plan was frozen before any v2 workload execution. It begins both implementations with a fresh full-seed database and the normal10,000-user warmup, then probes160,000 first; a valid SLO failure leads to80,000 and bisection. No old result is reused. Each implementation keeps one continuous app/database session through its search and300-second hold.

This shared ARM64 Mac/Docker accounting policy is a local diagnostic protocol. It does not establish native qualification when the native CPU guard flags a phase, and it cannot establish the official dedicated x86 leaderboard rank.
